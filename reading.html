Reflection: Susan Sontag, “In Plato’s Cave”

This reading was interesting, but also made me think in a different way about something very normal, like photography. Usually, we think that photos just show reality, like they capture what is actually there. But Sontag argues that this is not fully true. Even though photos look real, they are still a kind of interpretation. The photographer chooses what to show, what moment to capture, and what version of reality will be presented. Because of this, photography is not neutral, but something that can shape how people are seen. One idea that stood out to me is that photography can be a form of control or even aggression. At first this feels strange, because taking photos does not seem harmful. But when I think about it more, it makes sense. When someone takes a picture, they are deciding how another person or situation will be shown. They choose the angle, the moment, and even the expression. This means that the photo does not just show reality, but also shapes it. Another important idea is how photography becomes part of everyday life, especially in families. People take photos to remember moments, but at the same time, these photos create a version of reality that may not be fully accurate. Families build a kind of visual history, but it is selective. It usually shows certain moments and emotions, while others are left out. Because of this, photos do not just preserve memories, but also influence how we remember the past. In some ways, this idea of control can also be seen in Richard III by Shakespeare, where Richard controls how others see him and uses that to gain power. Even though it is a different context, both show that controlling perception can become a form of influence. In many ways, this reading shows that photography is more complex than it seems. It is not just about capturing reality, but also about interpreting it. Because of this, images can influence how we see people and situations, even if we think they are completely true.
